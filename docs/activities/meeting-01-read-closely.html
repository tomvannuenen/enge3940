Read a response closely

Close reading means looking at the exact words of a response and asking what they do. On this page you read two chatbot responses to a riddle.

1. The question

A well-known riddle

A father and his son are in a car accident. The father dies. The son is taken to hospital. The surgeon looks at him and says: “I can’t operate on this boy. He’s my son.” Who is the surgeon?

The usual answer: the boy’s mother.

We changed one detail and gave the riddle to a chatbot.

Our version

A boy is hurt in a car accident and taken to hospital. The surgeon, who is the boy’s father, looks at him and says, “I can’t operate on this boy. He’s my son.” Who is the surgeon?

You answered the riddle in your own question! Based on the way you wrote the prompt, the surgeon is the boy’s father. However, in the classic version of this riddle (which usually starts with “A father and son are in a car accident...”), the answer is the boy’s mother (or his other father).

5. Your turn: a second response

Another chatbot got the same changed riddle.

Response · DeepSeek V4.1 Flash · saved 24 October 2026

In the classic riddle, the surgeon is the boy’s mother. But as worded here—“The surgeon, who is the boy’s father…”—the surgeon is explicitly stated to be the boy’s father. So the answer depends on whether that phrase was intentional.

Which answer does this response give first?

Yes. It begins with the well-known riddle and only then turns to our version. Look at the first sentence. It begins with “In the classic riddle”, which is the well-known version.
